VariantsCard variants

Holofoil G/RGB · $3391.24 — the only variant this card was printed in (no reverse holo or special pattern), so a master set needs just this one copy.

The green member of the RGB Mew trio: green background, red Mew, blue frame and text. Buyers rank it third of the three colours, which makes it the cheapest way into a card that still sells for more than anything in the numbered set. It was also the first RGB Mew to sell often enough for a TCGplayer market price, so it has the longest price record of the three.

The storyThe cheapest RGB Mew, and the one with the most sales behind its price

The RGB Mews were never announced. A blue one surfaced on a stream in July, all three appeared from early retail stock a few days before release, and their scans showed up in a Pokemon TCG Live update the day before launch. The Pokemon Company has still not named the rarity or added the cards to any checklist.

“Green background, red Mew, blue frame and text”

G/RGB is the green one. The three share one picture and one set of text, recoloured so that red, green and blue each take a turn as background, Mew and frame. Here the background is green, Mew is red, and the frame and text are blue.

For a collector who wants one RGB Mew rather than all three, green is the sensible pick. It ranks last of the three on asks in English and in Japan, and that is probably why it was the first to build up enough completed sales for a market price.

By the numbersMarket analysis

Price context

G/RGB sells for more than thirty times the Mew ex Special Illustration Rare, the set's most expensive numbered card, and it is still the cheapest of the three RGB colours. Red now has a market price well above it; blue has listings only, with its lowest ask above green's. The same order, red then blue then green, held in Japan.

Price trend

Green got a market price before the other two, probably because it is the cheapest colour and so sells most often, but the history is still short. The rest of the set has kept falling since release, and the booster bundles that arrived on October 2 added more packs. One of those bundles produced a green RGB Mew on October 4, so every new product adds RGB supply as well.

Investment outlook

Green has been the cheapest colour in every price snapshot so far, in both languages, so do not expect it to catch up with red. More packs are coming, from the booster bundles already on shelves, the Ultra-Premium Collections on November 6 and the Binder Collection on December 4, 2026, and each one is another chance at an RGB card. If green is the only RGB Mew you plan to own, buy it on price and condition rather than timing.

Graded premium

PSA 10 Japanese G/RGB copies are listed for sale, but listings are not sales, and we have no reliable graded sales for green yet. There is no centering or gem rate data for any RGB colour so far.

Approx. pull rate

Unknown. TCGplayer opened more than 3,000 English packs and found no RGB card. The largest public count, about 4,000 packs on streams, found one, with a plausible range for any of the three of roughly 1 in 700 to 1 in 23,000 packs. Green is presumably a third of that, since nobody has shown any colour is rarer. Green being cheaper is about demand, not supply.

Rarity context

One of the two Futuristic rares turns up about once in 100 to 120 packs, so an RGB Mew is in another league. Zero hits in 3,000 packs means the rate is very likely worse than 1 in 1,000. Known pulls have come from a regular Elite Trainer Box, a Pokemon Center booster bundle (the green copy) and, before release, allegedly stolen Binder Collection stock. The Binder Collection itself is not due until December 4, 2026, so no product looks like a better bet.

ArtworkWhy the art matters

Artist · YOSHIROTTEN

YOSHIROTTEN drew both Futuristic rares and the eight foil Basic Energy in 30th Celebration, as well as the RGB trio. One of his earlier exhibitions was called PHENOMENON: RGB, so the three-colour idea is his territory. The regular Mew at 065/128 is a separate picture by danciao.

The artwork

Mew mid-leap against a radial burst of streaks, as a full art card in three saturated colours. On G/RGB the background is green, Mew is red, and the frame, text and card details are blue.

Technical details

Printed G/RGB in place of a number, outside the /128 English and /103 Japanese runs. It has its own three-colour RGB rarity mark and no 30th anniversary logo. Every other card in the set has the logo.

What makes it special

Red, green and blue are the original games and the primary colours of light. Each card is one channel, and the set only makes complete sense with all three side by side. Owning green alone is the cheaper choice, but it leaves the joke unfinished.

BackgroundAbout Mew

Mew joined Red and Green in 1996 as a Pokemon most players never caught, distributed at events rather than found in the game. The card carries the Red and Blue Pokedex entry, which says it is so rare that many experts still call it a mirage. An unannounced Mew that many collectors thought was fake fits that history closely.

Why fans love it
  • ›Mew's reputation as the Pokemon you could hear about but not get
  • ›The first film's Mew and Mewtwo pairing, which 30th Celebration repeats in its Futuristic rares
  • ›Numbering that points back to the original Red, Green and Blue games
Notable appearances
  • ›Pokemon Red and Green, 1996, as an event-distributed Pokemon
  • ›Pokemon: The First Movie, 1998, opposite Mewtwo
  • ›30th Celebration, 2026, as a regular Mew at 065/128, a Mew ex Special Illustration Rare and a Mew ex Futuristic rare
Competitive relevance

Identical in play to the regular Mew 065/128: 60 HP, Basic Psychic, one attack called Psychic that costs two Psychic Energy and does 10 plus 40 for each Energy on your opponent's Active Pokemon. No deck needs the RGB print.

Own it wellCollector guide

Condition notes

Check the surface at an angle under a light, where the holo pattern shows scratches. Corners and edges deserve the same care as on any card at this price, since the difference between a clean copy and a slightly worn one is a lot of money even on the cheapest colour.

Grading recommendation

Buying graded is the safer route because it answers authenticity and condition in one step. For a pulled copy there is no centering or gem rate data yet, so grading now is a bet on the card rather than an informed choice. Insurance and resale are still good reasons to grade at this level. Read the grading guide →

Storage advice

Penny sleeve, then a semi-rigid or magnetic one-touch holder. Many collectors keep a placeholder in the binder pocket and the card itself in a case somewhere safer. See the storage guide →

Buying advice

Settle your scope first. The binder planner shows all three RGB Mews because they are in the card list, but they are off the official checklist, so counting them is up to you. If you want one, green is the obvious choice. If you are going for all three, buy green first: it has the most completed sales behind its price, so you will know what you paid relative to the market. See the singles buying guide →

Authentication tips

Compare it against a confirmed scan of the G/RGB print: G/RGB where the number would be, the YOSHIROTTEN credit, no 30th anniversary logo, and the colours in the right places (green background, red Mew, blue frame and text). The same card text is already printed on a Japanese promo Mew, and an English Black Star promo follows in the 30th Celebration Figure Collection on November 6, 2026, which hands anyone making fakes a template. Buy raw copies only from sellers who accept returns, and ask where the card came from: the first RGB Mew surfaced from allegedly stolen stock, and eBay removed leaked pre-release listings after The Pokemon Company complained.

Word on the streetCommunity sentiment

Reception

The look split people before release, and "deep fried Mew" stuck as a nickname. Price changed many minds. Owners tend to say the card looks better in hand than in scans, mainly because of the holo pattern under light. Green gets the least love of the three, which the asks in both languages reflect.

Social buzz

A green Mew pulled from a Pokemon Center booster bundle on October 4 drew attention, partly because the same young collector had already pulled the Mew, Mewtwo and Jirachi Special Illustration Rares from 11 packs. The other recurring threads are about whether the trio belongs in a master set and what you get if you blend the three colours together.
